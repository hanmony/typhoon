1 Introduction
1.1 Background
Theworldwidefrequencyofseveremeteorologicaldisastersandtheextentoflosscaused by these disasters have increased significantly (Swiss 2015). Hurricane Katrina (2005) strucktheGulfCoastoftheUSAwithstrongwindsandstormsurges.Thedamagecaused by it was estimated to be approximately 108 billion USD. Hurricane Ike (2008) and
Hurricane Sandy (2012) hit the southern and eastern coasts of the USA and caused approximately 29.5 and 71.4 billion dollars in damages, respectively (Blake et al.
2007, 2013).
Therefore, the management and assessment of extreme meteorological disasters are essential to ensure the uninterrupted production of many industries and public works.
Many insurance companies have modified their insurance coverage and rates against natural disasters, and some companies have even gone bankrupt due to the unpredicted extent of losses caused by natural disasters (Watson and Johnson 2004).Therefore, reinsurersandinsurersshouldpreparefortheunanticipatedexpenditurestheymightincurdue to natural disasters. Since natural disasters significantly affect people and society, the government is paying considerable attention to their impact on public works. People are concerned as to how the government can assist them before and after a disaster and the government’s preparedness for future disasters. Therefore, the government should define areas prone to natural disasters and focus on how to respond to and recover from such events within a given budget (Sylves 2014). Moreover, buildinga communityresilient to natural disasters is imperative for quick recovery. Increased social risk perception raises
the probability of people taking preventive measures before a hurricane (Kim and Kang
2010; Comfort et al. 2012). Additionally, building a sustainable community is important for establishing land-use plans. Disaster mitigation, sustainability of a community, and land-useplanningarehighlycorrelated.Theextentoflifeandpropertylossessufferedby communities due to natural disasters would reduce if the government prepares a land-use plan.Suchplansshouldbebasedonaccurateriskassessmentsfordevelopingmorerobust programstomitigatetheeffectsofnaturaldisasters(Burby1998;Burbyetal.2000).The constructionindustry’sbuildingdesignstandardsandcodesshouldberevisedorupdatedto develop a sustainable built environment that is resistant to the increasing frequency of extreme weather events. The maintenance of lifelines, transportation systems, and infrastructure at the time of hurricane landing is extremely vital to reduce the extent of human and property damages. Therefore, a sophisticated natural disaster risk assessment
modelthat canbeappropriatelyadaptedtothechangingenvironmentisrequired (Bosher et al. 2007; Cruz and Okada 2008; Sussman and Freed 2008). Quantitative disaster managementandassessmentmethodsarenecessaryforsustainablesocialdevelopmentand protection of numerous industries against such major natural disasters.
1.2 Natural catastrophe model

Florida has developed the Florida Public Hurricane Loss Prediction Model (FPHLM) for hurricane damage assessments. This model is composed of three main modules: hazard module, vulnerability module, and finance module. The FPHLM assesses financial losses due to hurricanes for a given return period by utilizing numerous indicators such as occupancy, building inventory, and different typhoon features such as track, size, wind speed, and landfall. 1.3 Literature review
Previousstudiesontyphoondamagesfocusedonlythefeaturesofthetyphoon,whileless attention was paid to other indicators related to the typhoon damages, such as actions of exposure, vulnerability indicators, and hazard indicators (Kim et al. 2016). Park et al.
(2012)analyzedthetyphoondamagesanddamagetrendsbyutilizingatyphoonwindfield model. Park et al. (2011) predicted disaster areas and extent of damages using the strong windsaccompanyingtyphoonsasindicators.Kuetal.(2007)definedthecharacteristicsof typhoons in Korea and developed a statistical prediction model by utilizing rainfall data.
Shinetal.(2013)estimatedtyphoonpathsanddamagesbasedonthedataofthespeedand direction of typhoon movement. These studies on typhoon damages in Korea focused mainly on the features of typhoons, e.g., rainfall, wind speed, direction, and path.
Severalstudieshavehighlightedtheimportanceofunderstandingthebuiltenvironment vulnerability indicators that are associated with typhoon risks (Khanduri and Morrow
2003; Kim et al. 2016; De Silva et al. 2008). Typhoons with severe windstorms can, for example, cause huge financial damages to high-rise buildings (Khanduri and Morrow
2003).Furthermore, theextent oftyphoondamagescanvarydependingonwhether flood control structures are properly installed for the protection of communities against flood hazards (Brody et al. 2008). The cutting slopes of mountains or lands, formed due to construction activities or earthworks, may be associated with typhoon risks or damages since areas with steep slopes can be easily destroyed by typhoons (Dai et al. 2002; Zhai et al. 2007; Cui et al. 2009; Ayalew and Yamagishi 2005). To address the limitations of previous studies, this study investigates the characteristics of typhoon damages at the provincial level by developing a loss prediction model that utilizes the data of the features of typhoons (wind speed, wind speed radius, forward movement speed, and rainfall) and built environment vulnerability (number of high-rise buildings, complement rate of river, and number of cutting slopes). The typhoon damages were assessed by clustering the paths of types 1 and 2 typhoons. Furthermore, the relationships between the extent of damages and the risk indicators were examined to identifythedifferencesbetweentheindicatorsandthecorrespondingweightsofdifferent typhoontypes.Amultipleregressionanalysismethodwasutilizedtogeneratethebest-fit regression models for the paths of types 1 and 2 typhoons. These were used to comprehensively evaluate the relationship between the extent of loss and dependent vari-
ables. The framework and the findings of this study can be used as a reference for developing a typhoon damage prediction model for disaster management, public safety management, and managing extreme weather events in South Korea and other emerging
Asian countries.

5 Conclusion
Theoccurrenceofextremeweather-relatedcatastrophesandthecorrespondinglosseshave recently increased. Therefore, quantitative disaster management and assessment methods are essential for the development of a sustainable society that is resilient to such major natural disasters. Although the necessity of a loss assessment model for disaster management has increased, sufficient reliable data of damage caused by natural disasters and building inventory are not available for developing such models. Furthermore, synthetic studies on the characteristics of damage caused by typhoons along typhoon paths in emerging countries of Asia are limited.
Since the spatial resolution of the indicators used in this study was determined at the provincial level, it might not be easy to apply the findings and results of this study for estimatinglossestoindividualbuildingsfromtyphoons.Therefore,futureresearchshould focusonassessingtherelationshipbetweenlossestoindividualbuildingsandvariousbuilt environment vulnerability indicators to support the findings and results of this study.
